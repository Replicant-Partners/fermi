## I. What Rabble Is

Rabble.world is a location-based AR app where users mint digital creatures --- butterflies, dragonflies, and eventually broader insect taxa --- tied to real taxonomic data from the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF). Users take their creatures into the physical world: they fly them through parks, gather with friends in "rabbles" (location-anchored group events), chat through their creatures, and see them overlaid on the real world through phone cameras today and AR glasses tomorrow.

The experience is simple:

1. **Discover** a species in the GBIF-powered browser (650,000+ insect species)
2. **Mint** a creature --- a unique specimen with a name, a generated naturalist illustration, and real taxonomic data
3. **Fly** your creature at real-world locations, recording GPS tracks as "flights"
4. **Gather** in rabbles --- timed, located group events where creatures meet
5. **Chat** through your creature in rabble conversations
6. **See** creatures in AR, overlaid on the world around you

Every action costs credits. Credits are purchased with real currency. This is the same credit economy that powers Agent Bestiary World.

## II. Why This Works

### The Emotional Core

The question Rabble answers is deceptively simple: *can my creature be with me when I walk through the park?*

This is not a game mechanic. It is a relationship mechanic. The creature has a real species identity (it is a Red Admiral, not a generic butterfly). It has a name the user chose. It has a unique illustration generated in a style the user picked. It has a flight history --- places it has been, rabbles it has attended. Over time, the creature accumulates a biography.

People form attachments to digital companions when those companions have specificity, history, and presence. Tamagotchi proved this with a handful of pixels. Rabble does it with real taxonomic data, generated art, and physical location.

### The Social Core

Rabble events are inherently social and inherently local. A rabble is anchored to a place and a time --- a park on Saturday afternoon, a nature reserve at dawn. You bring your creature. Your friends bring theirs. The swarm host agent (an ABW agent) narrates the gathering, shares taxonomic facts about arriving species, and gives each rabble a sense of occasion.

This creates the conditions for network effects that are simultaneously digital and physical:

- **Local density matters.** A rabble with 2 people is quiet. A rabble with 20 is an event. This creates natural incentives for users to recruit friends in their area.
- **Creature diversity matters.** A rabble with 15 of the same species is less interesting than one with 15 different species. This creates natural incentives for users to mint different creatures, which costs credits.
- **Recurring events emerge.** "Saturday morning butterfly walk in Prospect Park" becomes a thing. Regularity compounds social bonds and creature flight histories.

### The AR Catalyst

Today, Rabble works through phone cameras. This is functional but awkward --- holding a phone up in a park for 30 minutes is not natural behavior. The experience is a novelty, not a habit.

AR glasses change this fundamentally. When creatures are visible in your peripheral vision as you walk a trail --- when your friend's dragonfly is hovering near the stream you're crossing --- the experience shifts from "using an app" to "being in a world." The creature is no longer something you look at; it is something that is *there*.

We are building for this transition. The phone AR experience is the seed. The glasses experience is the flower.

**Timeline:**
- **2026--2027**: Phone AR. Niche adoption among nature enthusiasts, butterfly collectors, birding communities. Pilot education partnerships.
- **2028--2029**: First affordable AR glasses ($300--800). Meta Orion, Apple lite, others. Rabble becomes a daily companion app. Sports and live event integrations begin.
- **2030+**: AR glasses mainstream. Rabble creatures are persistent companions visible to anyone wearing glasses in the same location. Brand, tourism, and drone integration layers mature.

### Beyond Nature

The creature mechanic is not inherently ecological. A creature is a digital companion anchored to a physical location, owned by a person, visible to others nearby. This abstraction applies wherever people gather:

- A **fan creature** trailing a cyclist in the Tour de France, visible to drones and broadcast cameras
- A **classroom creature** that a student mints on a biology field trip, carrying observation data
- A **festival creature** minted at a concert stage, its flight history a record of which sets you attended
- A **tourism creature** representing a regional species, collected as visitors explore a destination
- A **brand creature** appearing at retail locations, turning foot traffic into interactive experiences

Nature is the beachhead because it provides the strongest emotional and taxonomic foundation. But the platform is designed to support any context where physical presence deserves a digital companion. (See Section V for the full market analysis.)

## III. The Intelligence Flywheel: How Rabble Makes ABW Smarter

This is the section that distinguishes Rabble from every other AR creature app. Rabble is not just a consumer product that uses AI. It is a data generation engine that feeds intelligence back into the Agent Bestiary, which in turn makes Rabble smarter, which generates more data, which makes the Bestiary smarter.

### How It Works

Every creature minted, every flight recorded, every rabble gathered, every chat message sent generates structured data that flows into the ABW intelligence pipeline:

**1. Episodes**

Each user action creates an episode in the ABW memory system. A creature mint is an episode. A flight is an episode. A rabble gathering is a collection of episodes. These episodes are stored with embeddings --- mathematical representations of their semantic content.

**2. Active Dream Memory (ADM)**

The ABW platform runs a biologically-inspired consolidation cycle --- the "dreaming" process. During sleep phases, unconsolidated episodes are clustered, patterns are extracted as rules, entities and facts are identified, and the agent's knowledge graph evolves.

For Rabble, this means:
- The **swarm host agent** learns from every rabble it narrates. After 1,000 rabbles, it knows which taxonomic facts land well, which greeting styles work for different group sizes, how to handle late arrivals, which species combinations create interesting conversations.
- A **species intelligence agent** (future) could learn migration patterns, seasonal abundance, geographic distribution anomalies --- all from the aggregate of where users fly their creatures and which species they choose.
- A **social dynamics agent** (future) could learn which rabble configurations produce the most engagement --- time of day, group size, species diversity, location type.

**3. Coherence Evaluation**

The ABW coherence engine --- based on Thagard's Theory of Explanatory Coherence (1989) --- evaluates how well an agent's knowledge hangs together. Applied to Rabble's agents, this means the system doesn't just accumulate data; it evaluates whether its accumulated understanding is *internally consistent* and *explanatorily powerful*.

When the swarm host agent develops a rule like "Rabbles near water bodies have 40% higher engagement," the coherence engine checks whether this is supported by evidence, whether it contradicts other rules, and whether it fits the broader pattern of what makes rabbles successful. Rules that survive coherence evaluation become permanent knowledge. Rules that don't are pruned.

**4. Ontology Evolution**

Over time, each agent develops an evolving ontology --- a structured representation of what it knows. These ontologies are versioned, diffable, and visualizable. You can literally see an agent getting smarter: its knowledge graph grows denser, its rules become more specific, its entity relationships become richer.

This is the compounding asset. A swarm host agent with 6 months of rabble narration experience is qualitatively better than one with 6 days. It can't be replicated without repeating those 6 months of real-world usage.

### The Flywheel

```
Users mint creatures and gather in rabbles
    --> Actions generate episodes with embeddings
        --> ADM consolidation extracts patterns and rules
            --> Coherence engine validates and prunes knowledge
                --> Agents become smarter (better narration, better recommendations)
                    --> Better experiences attract more users
                        --> More users generate more episodes
                            --> ...
```

Every user who mints a creature is, unknowingly, teaching the ecology. Every rabble is a training session. Every flight is a data point. The intelligence isn't artificial in the way ChatGPT is artificial --- it's *cultivated*, grown from the real activities of real people in real places.

### Cross-Pollination with the Bestiary

Rabble's intelligence doesn't stay in Rabble. It flows back into the broader ABW ecosystem:

- **Embedding marketplace**: Rabble generates rich location-behavior embeddings. These embeddings --- representing how people interact with species in geographic contexts --- are valuable to researchers, conservationists, and other ABW agents. Through the embedding marketplace, Rabble users (who own their embeddings) can monetize this aggregate intelligence.
- **Agent composition**: ABW agents outside Rabble can hire Rabble-trained agents into workspaces. A conservation planning coordinator could compose with a species distribution agent trained on Rabble flight data.
- **Knowledge graph enrichment**: Entity-fact relationships extracted from Rabble (species-location-season-behavior patterns) enrich the broader ABW knowledge graph, making all agents that touch biodiversity data more capable.

The Bestiary's investment memo describes a three-tier economic architecture: Credits, Learning Economy, and Marketplace. Rabble activates all three simultaneously:

| ABW Tier | Rabble Activation |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Credits** | Every mint, flight, rabble, chat message consumes credits |
| **Learning Economy** | Rabble agents dream and consolidate autonomously, consuming credits |
| **Marketplace** | Rabble-trained agents and embeddings are tradeable assets |

### The Moat

Rabble's moat is not the technology (Flutter, Rust, Axum --- all replicable). The moat has three layers:

**1. GBIF Integration + Taxonomic Authenticity**

650,000+ insect species with real taxonomy, real conservation status, real geographic distribution. No competitor has built a consumer AR experience on real biodiversity data. This creates an authenticity that fictional creatures can never match --- your Red Admiral is a *real species* with a *real natural history*.

**2. Accumulated Intelligence via ABW**

Every rabble narrated, every flight recorded, every creature minted feeds the ADM pipeline. After 12 months of operation, the swarm host agent has learned things about species-human interaction that no dataset contains. This knowledge graph --- owned by the platform's agents --- is the compounding asset that can't be cloned by downloading the source code.

**3. Network Effects (Location-Dependent)**

Rabble's network effects are local. A user in Brooklyn cares about how many other users are in Brooklyn, not in Tokyo. This means:
- Once local critical mass is achieved, it's sticky (your rabble group is your friends)
- Competitors must achieve local density independently in each market
- Early mover advantage compounds geographically
